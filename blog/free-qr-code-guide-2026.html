What is a QR Code?

A QR (Quick Response) code is a 2D barcode that stores data — up to 4,296 characters. When scanned with a smartphone camera, it instantly performs an action: opens a URL, connects to WiFi, saves a contact, etc.

QR codes were invented in 1994 by Denso Wave (a Toyota subsidiary) for tracking car parts. They became popular for consumer use around 2010 and exploded again after 2020.

QR Code Best Practices for 2026

1. Keep the QR Code Simple

Don't add too much data — the more data, the denser the QR code, the harder to scan. For a URL, use a short link (bit.ly, etc.).

2. Use High Contrast

Dark QR code on light background. Black on white is best. Avoid low-contrast colors.

3. Leave a Quiet Zone

Keep at least 4 modules (small squares) of empty space around the QR code. Scanners need this margin.

4. Size Matters

For print, minimum 2 × 2 cm (0.8 × 0.8 in). For billboards, scale proportionally.

5. Test Before Printing

Always scan your QR code with multiple phones before printing 1,000 flyers.

6. Include a Call-to-Action

"Scan to view menu" is more effective than a bare QR code. Tell users what happens.

7. Use Dynamic QR Codes (If Available)

Dynamic QR codes let you change the destination URL without reprinting. Not all free tools support this, but it's worth checking.

8. Track Scans

Use UTM parameters in your URL to track which QR codes drive traffic.

How to Scan a QR Code

On modern smartphones (iOS 11+, Android 8+), you don't need an app:

  1. Open the camera app
  2. Point at the QR code
  3. Wait for a notification
  4. Tap the notification to open

On older phones, use a QR scanner app (many are free).

What's the maximum data a QR code can hold?

4,296 alphanumeric characters or 2,953 bytes. But for practical scanning, keep it under 300 characters.

Can I add a logo to the QR code?

Yes, but keep it small (under 30% of the QR code area) and test it with multiple phones.
