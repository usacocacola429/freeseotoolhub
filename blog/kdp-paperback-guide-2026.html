What is KDP Paperback Formatting?

KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing) is Amazon's self-publishing platform. When you publish a paperback, Amazon prints it on demand and ships it to readers. But for this to work, your PDF file must meet exact technical specifications:

  • Trim size — the physical dimensions of your book (e.g., 6" × 9")
  • Bleed — extra space beyond the page edge for images that extend to the edge
  • Margins — inside (gutter) and outside margins for readability and binding
  • Page count — affects spine width for the cover
  • Resolution — images must be at least 300 DPI

If any of these are wrong, KDP will reject your file. And fixing them manually in Word or InDesign can take hours.

Trim size is the physical size of your printed book. Amazon KDP supports many sizes, but these are the most popular:

For most first-time authors, 6" × 9" is the safest choice. It's the standard for nonfiction and works well for most genres.

Step 2: Set Up Your Margins (The Tricky Part)

Margins are where most authors fail. Amazon requires different margins depending on your page count:

Page Count Inside (Gutter) Margin Outside Margin Top/Bottom Margin
24–150 pages0.375"0.25"0.25"
151–300 pages0.5"0.25"0.25"
301–500 pages0.625"0.25"0.25"
501–700 pages0.75"0.25"0.25"
701–828 pages0.875"0.25"0.25"

Common Mistake

Many authors set the same margin on all sides. But Amazon requires a larger inside margin (gutter) because the binding takes up space. If you don't account for this, text gets cut off.

Step 3: Add Bleed (If You Have Images)

If your book has images that extend to the edge of the page (like a full-page illustration), you need bleed. This means adding 0.125" (3.2 mm) of extra space on the top, bottom, and outside edges.

  • No images extending to the edge? Skip bleed. Use "No Bleed" settings.
  • Full-page images? Add bleed. Your document size becomes trim size + 0.125" on three sides.

For example, a 6" × 9" book with bleed becomes 6.125" × 9.25".

Step 4: Format Your PDF

Once you know your trim size, margins, and bleed, you need to export a PDF that meets KDP's requirements:

  1. Use 300 DPI minimum for all images
  2. Embed all fonts (don't rely on system fonts)
  3. Use PDF/X-1a or high-quality print PDF preset
  4. Include page numbers on every page except the title page
  5. Keep file size under 650 MB

Common KDP Rejection Reasons (And How to Avoid Them)

1. Wrong Page Size

Your PDF page size must exactly match your chosen trim size. A 6" × 9" book must be 6" × 9" — not 6.01" × 9.01".

2. Text Too Close to the Edge

All text must be inside the safe margins. Anything outside gets cut off during printing.

3. Low-Resolution Images

Images below 300 DPI will look blurry. Amazon may reject them outright.

4. Missing Page Numbers

KDP doesn't require page numbers, but readers expect them. And if you're using a table of contents, you need them.

5. Fonts Not Embedded

If your PDF uses system fonts that aren't embedded, the printer will substitute them. Export with fonts embedded.
