Here's what internal links actually do:

  • Help Google understand site structure: Internal links tell Google which pages relate to each other and which are most important (linked most often).
  • Distribute page authority: A link from a high-authority page passes authority to the linked page — like an internal PageRank vote.
  • Speed up indexing: New pages get discovered faster when they're linked from existing, indexed pages.
  • Improve user experience: Readers find relevant content, stay longer, and read more pages — reducing bounce rate.
  • Increase pageviews: Good internal linking pushes users deeper into your site.

7 Internal Linking Best Practices for 2026

Follow these to get the most from your internal linking strategy:

1

Use Descriptive Anchor Text

Anchor text should describe what the linked page is about. Avoid generic phrases like "click here" or "read more" — they tell Google nothing.

2

Link Deep Pages, Not Just Homepage

Most sites over-link to their homepage. Instead, link to deep pages that need authority. Our tool finds these opportunities automatically — but complement it with our Website Crawler to spot orphan pages.

3

Keep Site Structure Flat (3-Click Rule)

Every important page should be reachable within 3 clicks from your homepage. This is easier with strong internal linking. Use our Website Crawler to visualize your site structure.

4

Link New Content to Old (and Vice Versa)

When you publish new content, link it to 3-5 older high-authority pages. Then edit older pages to link to the new content. This creates two-way authority flow. Our tool handles the "new content" part — the older edits are manual.

5

Vary Your Anchor Text

Don't use identical anchor text every time you link to a page. Vary it: sometimes keyword-focused, sometimes branded, sometimes conversational. This looks natural to Google.

6

Audit Broken Internal Links Regularly

Broken internal links waste crawl budget and frustrate users. Run our broken link audit monthly to catch and fix them.

7

Measure and Iterate

Use Google Search Console to track indexing and ranking improvements after adding internal links. Our GSC Visualizer helps you spot trends in your search performance.

Common Internal Linking Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-linking to the same page: If you link to your homepage 50 times, you dilute its signal.
  • Using the exact same anchor text every time: Looks spammy. Vary it.
  • Linking only in sidebars/footers: Contextual in-content links carry more weight than sidebar links.
  • Broken internal links: Wastes crawl budget. Use our broken link audit guide.
  • Linking orphan pages: If nothing links to a page, it may never rank. Fix with our tool.
  • Ignoring old content: Old posts can link to newer content to refresh their SEO value.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an internal link suggester?

An internal link suggester is a tool that analyzes your website's content and identifies relevant internal linking opportunities between pages. It suggests where to add internal links and what anchor text to use, improving your site's SEO structure and user navigation.

Why are internal links important for SEO in 2026?

Internal links help Google understand your site structure, distribute page authority (link equity), speed up indexing of new pages, reduce bounce rate, and increase pageviews. They're one of the most important on-page SEO factors you fully control.

Is there a free internal link suggester?

Yes. FreeSEOToolHub's Internal Link Suggester is 100% free with no signup, no credit card, and no usage limits. Paste your content and get linking suggestions instantly in your browser.

How many internal links should I have per page?

There's no strict rule, but best practice is 2-5 contextual internal links per 1,000 words for blog posts, and 5-15 links for pillar pages or category pages. Quality and relevance matter far more than quantity.

What's the best anchor text for internal links?

Use descriptive, keyword-rich anchor text that tells users and Google what the linked page is about. Avoid generic anchors like "click here". Vary your anchor text across pages to look natural and avoid over-optimization.

What's the difference between internal and external links?

Internal links point from one page on your site to another page on the same site — they distribute authority within your domain. External links point from your site to other websites — they build credibility but pass authority outside your site.
