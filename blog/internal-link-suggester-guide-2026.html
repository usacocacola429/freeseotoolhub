What is Internal Linking?

Internal linking means linking from one page on your website to another page on the same website. For example, if you write a blog post about "SEO basics", you might link to your "Meta Tag Generator" tool page.

It's different from external linking (linking to other websites) and backlinks (other websites linking to you).

Why Internal Linking Matters for SEO

1. It Helps Google Crawl Your Site

Google's bots follow links to discover new pages. If a page has no internal links pointing to it, Google might never find it — a problem called "orphan pages."

2. It Passes Page Authority

When you link from a high-authority page to a new page, you pass some of that authority. This helps new pages rank faster.

3. It Builds Topical Clusters

Google uses internal links to understand relationships between pages. If your "keyword density" tool page links to your "SEO guide" blog post, Google understands they're related.

4. It Improves User Experience

Visitors who find useful links stay longer. Longer sessions signal to Google that your content is valuable.

5. It Boosts Anchor Text Relevance

The anchor text (the clickable words) of internal links tells Google what the linked page is about. Using descriptive anchor text is a strong ranking signal.

Common Mistake

Many site owners use "click here" or "read more" as anchor text. This tells Google nothing. Use descriptive text like "free keyword density checker" instead.

How to Find Internal Linking Opportunities Manually

Before using a tool, it helps to understand the manual process:

  1. List your pages — every tool, blog post, and landing page
  2. Identify topics — what is each page about?
  3. Find related pages — which pages share keywords or themes?
  4. Add links — insert links in the body content, not just navigation
  5. Use descriptive anchor text — describe the destination, not "click here"

For a site with 10 pages, this is manageable. For a site with 100+ pages, it's overwhelming. That's where our free tool helps.

Internal Linking Best Practices for 2026

1. Link from High-Authority Pages

Your homepage and most-visited pages have the most authority. Link from them to new or important pages.

2. Use a "Hub and Spoke" Structure

Create a central "hub" page (like a pillar post) that links to related "spoke" pages. This builds topical authority.

3. Keep Links Relevant

Only link to pages that genuinely help the reader. Random links hurt trust and SEO.

4. Avoid Orphan Pages

Every page should have at least one internal link pointing to it. Use our tool to find pages with zero internal links.

5. Update Old Posts

Add links to new pages from older posts. This keeps your site fresh and helps new pages rank faster.

6. Use Breadcrumbs

Breadcrumb navigation (Home > Blog > SEO) helps users and Google understand your site structure.

7. Don't Overdo It

Too many internal links look spammy. Aim for 2–5 links per 1000 words.
