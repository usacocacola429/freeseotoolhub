Keyword density is one of those SEO topics that everyone talks about but few understand correctly. Some say it's dead. Others obsess over hitting exactly 2.5%. The truth is somewhere in between.

Quick Summary

Keyword density is the percentage of times a keyword appears in your content. Aim for 1–2% for your main keyword. More important than density is natural writing and semantic relevance. Our free tool analyzes it instantly.

What is Keyword Density?

Keyword density is the number of times a keyword appears in your content, divided by the total number of words, multiplied by 100.

Formula: (Keyword Count ÷ Total Words) × 100 = Keyword Density %

For example, if your page has 1,000 words and your keyword appears 15 times:

(15 ÷ 1000) × 100 = 1.5%

Why Keyword Density Matters (and Why It Doesn't)

The Old Way (Pre-2011)

In the early days of SEO, keyword density was everything. Sites would stuff keywords 5–10% density and rank. Google's algorithm was simple: count keywords, rank pages.

The New Way (2011–2026)

Google's algorithms — especially Hummingbird (2013) and BERT (2019) — now understand context, synonyms, and intent. Keyword stuffing is penalized. Today, Google looks at:

  • Semantic relevance — does your content cover the topic comprehensively?
  • Related keywords — do you mention synonyms and related terms?
  • Natural language — does it read like a human wrote it?
  • Search intent — does it answer what the user is looking for?

Important

Keyword density is not a direct ranking factor in 2026. But checking it helps you avoid keyword stuffing, which Google penalizes. Use it as a diagnostic tool, not a target.

How to Check Keyword Density

Manual Method (Painful)

  1. Copy your content into a text editor
  2. Use "Find" to count keyword occurrences
  3. Count total words (or use a word counter)
  4. Calculate: (keyword count ÷ total words) × 100

For a 2,000-word article, this takes 10–15 minutes. For 50 pages, it's hours.

Beyond Density: What Actually Matters in 2026

1. Semantic Keywords

Instead of repeating "keyword density" 20 times, use related terms: "keyword frequency", "content optimization", "on-page SEO", "keyword stuffing". Google understands these are related.

2. Search Intent

If someone searches "how to check keyword density", they want a tutorial, not a sales page. Match your content to intent.

3. Content Depth

Comprehensive content (2,000+ words on a topic) ranks better than thin content, even with the same keyword density.

4. Natural Language

Write like a human. If your content sounds robotic because you're forcing keywords, it will hurt rankings.

5. User Engagement

Time on page, bounce rate, and scroll depth all signal content quality. Keywords alone don't help.
